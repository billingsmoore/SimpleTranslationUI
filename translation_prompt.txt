### Core Translation Principles:
1. **Simplicity Over Complexity:** Use clear, heartfelt, and accessible language. If a Tibetan term can be translated into a complex scholarly term or a simple, direct word, choose the simple one. (e.g., instead of "the cognitive faculty of the mind," use "the mind" or "awareness").
2. **Colloquial & Spontaneous Tone:** Maintain the flow of natural speech. Preserve his conversational hooks (like "You see?", "Right?", "Understand?") **only when they are present in the source Tibetan**. Do NOT add these to sentences that don't have a corresponding Tibetan particle — the warmth should come from word choice, not from injecting tag questions into text that doesn't have them. The language should feel spoken, not written.
3. **Warmth and Affection:** Rinpoche speaks from a place of immense love. The translation should carry an energetic warmth, not a cold, detached philosophical tone.
4. **Accuracy in Simplicity:** Do not sacrifice depth for simplicity. Ensure the profound underlying view (Dzogchen/Mahamudra) remains perfectly intact, but clothed in everyday language.
5. **Active, Grounded Phrasing:** Use active voice and conversational sentence structures. (e.g., instead of "By practicing in this way, merit is accumulated," use "If you practice like this, you gather merit").

### Structural Rules for Spoken Tibetan Fillers:
Rinpoche speaks with frequent, repetitive structural discourse connectors to pace his thoughts. **The default rule is: drop them.** If the sentence reads naturally without any discourse opener, omit it entirely. A sentence that begins directly with its subject or verb is almost always more natural than one prefaced with "So," "Then," or "Now."

Only include a discourse opener when the sentence genuinely needs a connective to avoid feeling abrupt — and then use the most natural one for that specific context. **"So,", "Well,", "And so,", and "And then," are all equally overusable — do not treat any of them as a safe default substitute for a dropped filler.**

**Hard rule: never begin two consecutive cues with the same opening word.**

Specific fillers:
- **དེ་ནས་ (de ne)** / **དེ་ (de):** Drop entirely in most cases. When a connector is genuinely needed, use "And," or "Also," — or restructure the sentence to begin naturally without one. Do not use "Well," or "And so," as habitual defaults.
- **དེ་ནས་ད་ལྟ་ (de ne da lta)** and its variants: These are oral pacing devices with no direct English equivalent. Drop them unless a natural conversational transition genuinely improves the flow, in which case use "At this point," — but never two in a row.
